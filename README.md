# MathHeroRPG - Gameplay Concept

## Core Idea

MathHeroRPG is a math practice game presented as an RPG.

The player chooses which area of mathematics they want to practice. Mathematical areas are separated into different regions on the world map.

There are no requirements to enter a mathematical area.

---

## World Structure

Each mathematical field has its own area on the map.

Examples:

* Arithmetic Forest
* Algebra Ruins
* Trigonometry Caverns
* Geometry Mountains

Each mathematical field can contain multiple smaller areas with their own:

* Enemies
* Environment
* Rewards
* Bosses
* Gameplay twists

Completing an area can unlock another area within the same mathematical field.

---

## Game Modes

Every area has two modes:

### Practice

The player chooses the difficulty of the problems they want to solve.

Practice is intended for directly studying or practicing a specific difficulty.

Rewards:

* XP
* Gold
* Low chance of rare cosmetics

Practice does not provide equipment.

### Adventure

Adventure is the main RPG progression mode.

The player starts with lower-difficulty enemies and progressively encounters harder problems.

The difficulty is progressed systematically rather than chosen freely.

Rewards:

* XP
* Gold
* Equipment
* Cosmetics

Adventure areas contain enemies, progression paths, and bosses.

---

## Area Progression

Each mathematical area has its own progression and level.

There is no single global player level.

Example:

```text
Arithmetic Forest       Level 24
Algebra Ruins           Level 12
Geometry Mountains      Level 18
Trigonometry Caverns    Level 7
```

Progress in one mathematical field does not affect access to another field.

The player can focus entirely on one mathematical area if they want.

---

## Areas and Unlocks

A mathematical field can contain multiple connected areas.

Example:

```text
Trigonometry Caverns
    |
    +-- Cave Entrance
    |
    +-- Crystal Depths
    |
    +-- Hydra's Nest
    |
    +-- Trigonometric Abyss
```

Completing an area can unlock the next area within that mathematical field.

New areas can introduce:

* New enemies
* New problem types
* Different difficulty progression
* New equipment
* New cosmetics
* Unique gameplay mechanics

---

## Bosses

Bosses represent major challenges within an area.

Boss fights can contain unique mechanics instead of simply having more HP.

Examples:

* Different parts of a boss represent different math topics.
* Certain attacks require solving a specific type of problem.
* Boss phases introduce progressively harder problems.
* Special mechanics can change how problems are presented.

---

## Titles

Each mathematical area has level-based titles that can be unlocked as collectibles.

Examples:

```text
Level 5   - Angle Adept
Level 10  - Trigonometric Scholar
Level 20  - Master of Trigonometry
Level 30  - Master of Polygon Magic
```

Titles can be equipped by the player and displayed with their character.

Titles provide a long-term collection goal without affecting the player's mathematical progression.

---

## Progression Philosophy

The game separates mathematical practice from RPG progression.

Practice gives the player immediate control over what difficulty they want to work on.

Adventure provides a structured progression through increasingly difficult mathematics.

The player is never forced to complete easier mathematical fields before accessing harder ones.
